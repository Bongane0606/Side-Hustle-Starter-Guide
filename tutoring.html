Tutoring business

Subjects, lessons,
parents, progress,
and steady packages.

This guide shows how to start tutoring without chasing once-off lessons forever. Package your knowledge, communicate clearly with parents, and prove progress.

What you sell

Tutoring is confidence and progress

Customers pay because a learner is struggling, preparing for exams, trying to improve marks, or needs structure. Your job is to make learning clearer and give parents confidence that the learner is moving forward.

You can tutor Maths, English, Accounting, Physical Sciences, Life Sciences, languages, reading, homework support, computer basics, music, or study skills.

Starter offer example:

Grade 10 to 12 Maths support. One free 20-minute assessment, then a four-week package with one lesson per week, homework tasks, and parent feedback after each session.
Offer roadmap

Package your time.

Tutoring has low startup costs, but your income disappears if every lesson is a once-off. Packages create commitment and make planning easier.

Exact launch steps

How to start from zero

  1. Choose subject and grade range

    Start with the subjects where you can explain clearly and confidently. A narrow offer is easier for parents to trust.

  2. Create a simple assessment

    Prepare 5 to 10 questions that show the learner's level. Use the assessment to explain what the learner needs and what your package will cover.

  3. Build a lesson structure

    Each session should have revision, teaching, practice, correction, and homework. Structure makes lessons feel professional.

  4. Sell to parents and learners nearby

    Start with schools, neighbours, family groups, churches, community boards, and WhatsApp groups. Ask for permission before posting at schools.

  5. Track attendance and progress

    Keep a register, topics covered, homework given, and marks if available. Send short parent updates after lessons.

  6. Move good learners into packages

    After the assessment, offer a four-week or term package. Packages reduce cancellations and help learners build routine.

Pricing

Do not only sell one hour

Direct costs

  • Worksheets and printing
  • Data for online lessons
  • Transport if in person
  • Stationery and past papers

Time costs

  • Lesson preparation
  • Teaching time
  • Marking and feedback
  • Parent communication

Profit

  • Owner payment
  • Better materials
  • Online tools or data
  • Emergency cancellation buffer

Quote formula: preparation time + lesson time + materials + transport/data + profit. A package price should reward consistency and protect your calendar.

Policies

Parents need clarity

Cancellation

Set how many hours' notice is needed. Missed lessons without notice should not automatically be replaced for free.

Payment

Monthly or four-week packages should be paid before lessons start. This protects your schedule.

Results

Do not promise marks you cannot control. Promise structure, support, feedback, and effort tracking.
