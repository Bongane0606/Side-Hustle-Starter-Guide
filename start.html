Start here

Your first business,
one step at a time.

This page is for matriculates, school leavers, and anyone who has never started a business before. Follow the steps in order before spending serious money.

Step 01

Choose one problem to solve

Do not start with "I want money." Start with a customer problem you can solve. A business survives when people pay because your offer saves them time, helps them look good, feeds them, teaches them, or makes an event easier.

  • Hair: "People need neat styles for school, work, and events."
  • Catering: "Families need reliable food for gatherings."
  • Tutoring: "Learners need help passing specific subjects."
  • Decor: "Families need events to look organised without doing everything themselves."
Step 02

Describe your first customer

A beginner mistake is saying "everyone is my customer." Be specific. Your first customer might be a neighbour, a church member, a parent at school, a classmate, a salon owner, or someone planning a funeral or birthday.

Good example: "Parents of Grade 10 to 12 learners in my area who need Maths help every Saturday."
Weak example: "Anyone who needs tutoring."
Step 03

Test demand before buying everything

Before spending, ask at least 10 real people if they would pay for your offer. Show photos, a sample plate, a price list, a mock table setup, or a trial lesson. Your goal is to hear useful truth, not only compliments.

  • Ask: "Would you pay R___ for this?"
  • Ask: "What would stop you from booking?"
  • Ask: "When would you need this service?"
  • Ask: "Can I message you when bookings open?"
Step 04

Price using costs, time, and profit

Your price must cover materials, transport, airtime/data, electricity or gas, cleaning, packaging, helpers, and your time. Profit is not what is left by accident; it must be planned into the price.

Simple formula: materials + transport + helper/time cost + small profit = minimum selling price.
Step 05

Start with a small package

Beginners often try to look big too early. A small package is easier to explain, easier to price, and easier to deliver well. Once people trust you, you can add more options.

  • Hair: 3 styles only.
  • Catering: 2 dishes and one order day.
  • Tutoring: 1 subject and one grade range.
  • Decor: 10-person table setup or one backdrop package.
Step 06

Protect yourself with deposits and records

Use deposits for bookings that require stock, travel, or reserved time. Keep a record of every cost and every payment. This protects your business from cancellations, missing items, and confusion.

  • Write customer name, phone number, date, service, price, deposit, and balance.
  • Send the customer a clear WhatsApp message confirming what is included.
  • For decor, count and photograph items before delivery and after collection.
