Catering business

Plates, trays,
events, and food
that makes profit.

This guide shows how to start home catering without cooking too many items, underpricing ingredients, or losing money on containers and delivery.

What you sell

Catering is food plus reliability

Customers pay for taste, clean preparation, correct portions, timing, and confidence that the food will arrive as promised. A small menu that you cook well is stronger than a large menu that stresses you out.

You can start with Friday plates, lunch packs, family trays, Sunday meals, funeral food, birthday platters, office lunches, school lunch boxes, or weekend event orders.

Starter offer example:

Friday plate menu with two mains, one starch, one salad, and one delivery window. Orders close Thursday evening. Customers pay upfront or pay a deposit for 10+ plates.
Menu roadmap

Cook narrow before you cook big.

Food businesses fail when the menu becomes too wide. Start with dishes that share ingredients and can be portioned consistently.

Exact launch steps

How to start from zero

  1. Choose your first menu

    Pick two or three dishes you can cook the same way every time. Avoid dishes with expensive ingredients until customers are already ordering.

  2. Cook a test batch

    Measure ingredients, count portions, and record the real cost. Include oil, spices, gas or electricity, containers, serviettes, and transport.

  3. Get honest tasting feedback

    Give small testers to people who will be honest. Ask about taste, portion size, price, packaging, and whether they would buy again.

  4. Open pre-orders

    Post the menu, price, order deadline, payment method, delivery area, and collection time. Pre-orders stop you from buying too much food.

  5. Cook with a checklist

    Write the order list, quantities, customer names, delivery route, and payment status. Do not rely on memory on a busy cook day.

  6. Review profit after delivery

    Count income, subtract every cost, and record leftover stock. Adjust portions, pricing, or menu before the next order day.

Pricing

Every plate must carry its costs

Food costs

  • Meat, starch, vegetables
  • Oil, spices, sauces
  • Containers and packaging
  • Waste and test portions

Operating costs

  • Gas or electricity
  • Transport or delivery
  • Cleaning supplies
  • Airtime and data

Profit

  • Your cooking time
  • Owner payment
  • Equipment savings
  • Emergency stock fund

Quote formula: ingredients + packaging + cooking cost + delivery + labour time + profit. If the customer wants delivery, extra meat, late changes, or event service, the price must increase.

Policies

Food bookings need rules

Order deadline

Close orders before buying ingredients. Late orders should only be accepted if you can still deliver quality.

Deposit

Take deposits for family trays and event orders. Large orders should not be cooked from your own pocket.

Delivery

Set delivery areas and times. Delivery is a cost, not a favour, and it must be included in the quote.
