Pick your lane

Four hustles. One method.

Each one has different tools and a different first customer, but the same three questions decide if it survives: what do you sell, what does it cost you, and who pays first.

01 / Hair

Braiding & styling

Low stock cost, income tied to your hands and your hours.

  • Home or rented chair
  • Reference photos as your menu
  • Repeat clients = real income
From R770
02 / Catering

Home catering

Cook what you already cook well, sell it by the plate or the tray.

  • Test batch before big orders
  • Container & delivery costs
  • Weekend events pay best
From R950
03 / Tutoring

Tutoring & lessons

Sell knowledge you already have — maths, English, an instrument, a language.

  • No stock costs at all
  • Parents pay per term, not per lesson
  • Group lessons scale your hour
From R150
04 / Reselling

Reselling & thrifting

Buy in bulk or bale, sell per item — clothes, sneakers, airtime, data.

  • Cash flow is the whole game
  • Photos sell before quality does
  • Turnover beats markup
From R500
How to use this site

Three steps, in order.

  1. Read the deep-dive for your hustle

    Every hustle in the guide has real starting costs, a launch checklist, and the mistakes people usually make in month one.

  2. Fill in the ledger as you read

    The business plan template does the maths for you — startup costs, pricing, and break-even — as you type.

  3. Print it or download it

    Take the finished page to a stokvel, a family meeting, or a bank — as a PDF or a Word document.

Proof from small starts

Success stories

These examples show the pattern: start narrow, price properly, track the money, and reinvest before you expand.

Hair · Soweto

Three bookings became weekends full

She started with discounted photo clients, then moved to deposits for every long install.

First month: R2,400 revenue

Catering · Durban

One dish, one order day

Instead of a huge menu, he sold plates every Friday and used pre-orders to buy ingredients.

Break-even: 18 plates

Tutoring · Polokwane

Parents paid per term

She switched from single lessons to four-week packages and stopped losing income to cancellations.

Best move: group lessons

Reselling · Cape Town

Fast turnover beat big markup

Small clothing batches, clear photos, and daily posts helped stock move before cash got stuck.

Restock cycle: 10 days
