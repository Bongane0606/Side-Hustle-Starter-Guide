01 / Hair

Braiding & styling

Start from R770

Your income is tied to your two hands and how many chairs you can fill in a week. Keep stock costs low until you have repeat clients.

ItemOnce-off / MonthlyCost
Hair, extensions & braiding thread (first batch)Once-offR450
WhatsApp Business profile + price list photosOnce-offR0
Flyers, 100 copiesOnce-offR120
Chair rental at a friend's salon, 1 weekMonthlyR200
Total to startR770

Launch checklist

  1. Photograph 6–8 styles you can actually do well, in good light.
  2. Set 3 prices: quick style, medium, full install — write them down.
  3. Post the price list in a local WhatsApp/Facebook group.
  4. Book your first 3 clients at a discount in exchange for photos.
  5. Buy stock per booking, not in bulk, until demand is steady.

The one that kills it

  • Doing "friend prices" for everyone — you run out of stock money by week three.
  • No fixed prices, so every booking becomes a negotiation.
  • Not asking for a deposit on long installs, then the client no-shows.

02 / Catering

Home catering

Start from R950

Sell what you already cook well, by the plate or the tray. Weekend events pay more per hour than daily plate sales — but daily sales build your name.

ItemOnce-off / MonthlyCost
Test batch ingredients (2 dishes)Once-offR250
Take-away containers, 50 packMonthlyR180
Gas / electricity top-up for cook daysMonthlyR220
Menu card + flyersOnce-offR150
Delivery — own transport or taxi fareMonthlyR150
Total to startR950

Launch checklist

  1. Pick 2–3 dishes you can cook consistently, not your whole repertoire.
  2. Cook a test batch, price it per portion including your time.
  3. Get 5 honest tasters — neighbours, church, work — before selling.
  4. Open orders for one day a week only, to control your stock buying.
  5. Take a deposit for orders over 10 plates.

The one that kills it

  • Pricing the plate on ingredients only — forgetting gas, containers, and your own time.
  • Taking every order regardless of size, then running out of ingredients mid-week.
  • No deposit on large event orders, so a cancellation costs you the stock.

03 / Tutoring

Tutoring & lessons

Start from R150

No stock, no delivery — you're selling knowledge you already have. The trade-off is your income is capped by your hours, unless you teach groups.

ItemOnce-off / MonthlyCost
Printed worksheets / past papersOnce-offR80
Data for online sessionsMonthlyR70
Flyers at local schools / community boardOnce-offR0 (free permission)
Total to startR150

Launch checklist

  1. Pick 1–2 subjects and a grade range you're confident in.
  2. Set a per-lesson price and a cheaper per-term package price.
  3. Offer one free 20-minute assessment lesson to close new parents.
  4. Ask satisfied parents for a written reference you can share.
  5. Fill gaps in your week with a small group rate, not more solo hours.

The one that kills it

  • Charging per lesson only, so income disappears every school holiday.
  • No cancellation policy — students skip and you lose paid hours.
  • Turning every student into a one-on-one instead of grouping similar levels.

04 / Reselling

Reselling & thrifting

Start from R500

Clothes, sneakers, airtime, data — the whole game is cash flow. Turning your stock over fast beats holding out for a bigger markup.

ItemOnce-off / MonthlyCost
First bale or stock buyOnce-offR350
Photo backdrop / good lighting spotOnce-offR0
Data for posting dailyMonthlyR70
Packaging for deliveriesMonthlyR80
Total to startR500

Launch checklist

  1. Buy a small, focused stock batch — one category, not a bit of everything.
  2. Photograph every item the same way, same background, same light.
  3. Price to move: mark up 40–60%, not double, until you have buyers.
  4. Post daily in the same 2–3 groups so people expect to see you.
  5. Reinvest the first sales into stock before spending the profit.

The one that kills it

  • Buying variety instead of depth — one of everything sells slowly.
  • Spending profit before restocking, so momentum dies after batch one.
  • No record of what you paid per item — you can't tell if you're actually profiting.

05 / Event decor

Decor setup & rentals

Start from R1,500

Event decor can serve weddings, funerals, birthday parties, baby showers, graduations, church events, corporate events, lobola celebrations, and community gatherings. Start small with table styling and grow your inventory only when paid bookings prove demand.

ItemOnce-off / MonthlyCost
Basic table decor: runners, napkins, centrepieces, candles, small vasesOnce-offR600
Plates, glasses, cutlery sample set for photos and small bookingsOnce-offR450
Cleaning supplies, storage boxes, labels, repair kitOnce-offR250
Data, printed flyer, and WhatsApp catalogue photosOnce-offR200
Total to startR1,500

Launch checklist

  1. Choose one starter package: 10-person table setup, funeral home setup, or birthday backdrop.
  2. Create 3 sample looks at home and photograph them clearly: simple, elegant, and premium.
  3. Write a package list showing exactly what is included and what costs extra.
  4. Take a non-refundable booking deposit before buying or reserving any items.
  5. Inspect, count, clean, and photograph items before and after every event.

The one that kills it

  • Buying tents, chairs, tables, and stretch tents before you have regular bookings.
  • No breakage deposit, so missing glasses, plates, and chair covers come from your profit.
  • Quoting without delivery, setup time, cleaning time, transport, and assistant costs.
