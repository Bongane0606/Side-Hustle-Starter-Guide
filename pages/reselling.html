Reselling business

Stock, photos,
turnover, delivery,
and cash flow.

This guide shows how to start reselling without trapping all your money in slow stock. Buy focused, photograph clearly, price to move, and keep records.

What you sell

Reselling is buying well and moving fast

Customers pay because you found something useful, affordable, stylish, scarce, or convenient. Your job is to choose the right stock, present it clearly, answer fast, and deliver honestly.

You can resell thrift clothes, sneakers, handbags, beauty products, phone accessories, data or airtime, school supplies, baby items, small electronics, or seasonal products.

Starter offer example:

One focused batch: 15 clean thrift tops in popular sizes. Each item has a clear photo, size, condition, price, delivery option, and payment rule. No holding without deposit.

Exact launch steps

How to start from zero

  1. Choose one product category

    Pick a category you understand and can inspect properly. Clothing sizes, sneaker condition, and electronics quality must be described honestly.

  2. Set a buying limit

    Decide the maximum you can spend without needing that money for transport, food, or school needs. Do not gamble all your savings on batch one.

  3. Photograph every item clearly

    Use the same background and light. Show front, back, close-up, size, flaws, and packaging. Good photos reduce repeated questions.

  4. Post with full details

    Every post should include price, size, condition, location, delivery cost, payment method, and whether you accept holds.

  5. Track each item

    Record buying cost, selling price, delivery cost, buyer name, date sold, and profit. Without item records, you cannot tell what is working.

  6. Reinvest before spending

    Put aside money for the next batch before using profit personally. Reselling dies when sales happen but stock cannot be replaced.

Pricing

Price for turnover and profit

Direct costs

  • Item buying price
  • Transport to supplier
  • Cleaning or repairs
  • Packaging

Selling costs

  • Data for posting
  • Delivery or courier
  • Discounts and bundles
  • Payment fees if any

Profit

  • Owner payment
  • Restock money
  • Emergency fund
  • Supplier search costs

Quote formula: buying price + transport + cleaning/packaging + selling cost + profit. A smaller profit on fast-moving stock can beat a big markup that sits for months.

Policies

Clear rules stop confusion

Holding items

Do not hold items for days without a deposit. Holding blocks other buyers and slows cash flow.

Condition

Describe flaws honestly. A small flaw disclosed early is better than an angry customer later.

Returns

Set a return rule before selling. For thrift items, make sizing and condition clear so buyers understand what they are accepting.
