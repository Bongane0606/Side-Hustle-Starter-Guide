Hair business

Braids, styling,
wigs, washes,
and repeat clients.

This guide shows how to start a hair business without overbuying stock or giving away your time. Start with the styles you can deliver neatly, then build a booking system that protects your money.

What you sell

Hair is skill, trust, and time

Customers pay for neat work, clean tools, honest timing, and a style that lasts. Your product is not only the final hairstyle; it is also the booking experience, the advice you give, and how reliable you are.

You can start with braiding, cornrows, straight back, knotless braids, wig installation, wash and blow, natural hair styling, children’s styles, or simple event styling.

Starter offer example:

Three styles only: straight back, medium knotless, and cornrows. Customer sends reference photo, pays deposit for long styles, and buys own hair unless stock is included in the package.
Tools and stock

Buy what earns this week.

Hair stock can trap money quickly. Own your basic tools, but buy extensions and special products per booking until demand is steady.

Exact launch steps

How to start from zero

  1. Choose three styles

    Pick styles you can do neatly and consistently. Do not advertise every style you have ever tried. Beginners build trust by being reliable, not by offering everything.

  2. Create a photo menu

    Do two or three discounted model clients and take clear photos from the front, back, side, and close-up. Write the price and estimated time next to each style.

  3. Set booking rules

    Long styles need a deposit. Late arrivals lose time. Cancellations must be communicated early. Tell clients whether hair is included or not before they arrive.

  4. Sell to nearby people first

    Start with schoolmates, neighbours, church members, relatives, local salons, and WhatsApp groups. Ask every happy client for permission to post their photo.

  5. Track time and profit per style

    Write down how long each style takes, what stock was used, what the client paid, and how much profit remained. Raise prices on styles that take too long for too little profit.

  6. Build repeat bookings

    Message clients after two to four weeks for maintenance, take-down, wash day, or a new style. Repeat clients make the business stable.

Pricing

Charge for stock and hours

Direct costs

  • Hair or extensions
  • Gel, mousse, oil, shampoo
  • Transport or chair rental
  • Electricity, water, towels, cleaning

Time costs

  • Consultation messages
  • Parting and preparation
  • Actual styling hours
  • Clean-up and photo time

Profit

  • Owner payment
  • Tool replacement
  • Product restock
  • Saving for chair or salon space

Quote formula: stock + products used + chair/transport cost + your hourly time + profit. If a style takes six hours, the price must respect six hours.

Policies

Booking rules protect your day

Deposit

Take deposits for long styles, styles needing special stock, and weekend bookings. The deposit confirms the appointment.

Late arrival

Set a grace period. If one client is very late, the rest of your day can collapse.

Hair included

Always state whether hair is included, how many packets are needed, and which colours are available.
